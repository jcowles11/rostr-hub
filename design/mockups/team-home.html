What moved where

  • Next game keeps the focal position, with a visible Game Day Checklist (roster → lineup → print) — the daily-use workflow is literally on the screen.
  • "Needs Attention" replaces B's evaluation coverage. Cards reflect daily decisions: unassigned players, practices missing plans, lineups not built. These are always present in normal coaching.
  • Header pill becomes "2 to do" — a scannable count of Needs-Attention items. Amber if anything to do, green if clean.
  • Shortcuts reweighted: Game Day · Practices · Teams · Add Player are the primary 2×2. Score Entry + Rankings drop to a "seasonal" chip row.
  • Roster bar stays — it's context, always relevant.
  • When a tryout session is active, a thin banner could appear above Needs Attention: "Tryouts in progress → Score Entry" — surfacing Score Entry temporarily without making it a default fixture.

Why this works

  • A coach opening the app in March sees Game Day Checklist + This Week + Needs Attention — all relevant.
  • A coach opening the app in August pre-tryouts sees a "Tryouts in progress" banner + Score Entry surfaced temporarily.
  • No wasted pixels on evaluation UI during the 85% of the year it's unused.
  • The "what should I do right now" question is answered twice: globally (header pill), concretely (Needs Attention cards).

My honest take. Direction A is the safest and highest-reward-per-effort bet — it makes the product feel intentional without inventing new backend concepts. Direction B is the most differentiated but only if you're willing to invest in real readiness logic (evaluation coverage %, lineup-built status, etc). Direction C is the most opinionated — it assumes the coach wants a prioritized to-do list, which is a product claim worth testing with a real user before committing.

If you don't want to pick yet, we can mix: A's clean hero + B's readiness cards + C's "Today" list is actually coherent, and probably the strongest single direction.
