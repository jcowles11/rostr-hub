# Rostr — North Star

**Last updated:** May 3, 2026
**Audience:** founder + early advisors + would-be investors
**Status:** post-pilot architecture, pre-revenue, eight feature sprints ahead of the live pilot site behind feature flags

This doc is the strategic picture: what Rostr is, what it's worth, what's missing, and how it becomes a real business — not just a really good piece of high-school baseball software.

---

## 1. What Rostr is, in one sentence

Rostr is the **operating system for high-school sports programs** — and the **verified-athlete identity layer** that gets built as a side effect of running on it.

The order matters. The OS comes first. The identity layer is the by-product. The recruiter / scout / NIL / data-licensing business is the monetization of the by-product. Get the OS wrong and nothing else matters.

---

## 2. The core thesis (why this works when GameChanger doesn't)

Three observations:

1. **HS sports is the last large vertical without a real software stack.** Coaches still run programs out of clipboards, group texts, and Google Sheets. GameChanger owns the live-scorebook moment but doesn't touch tryouts, roster, practice, comms, or recruiting. Hudl owns video. Nobody owns the *day*.

2. **Trust in athlete data is the moat.** Recruiting is a $billion problem because every recruiter is reading self-reported PR9 numbers. The first platform with a credible *coach-verified* career record per athlete becomes the place colleges look first. The verification can't be retrofit — it has to be generated by the same coach, at the same desk, doing the same job they were already doing. That's the wedge.

3. **Parents pay for hope.** ~16,000 US high schools play baseball. ~480,000 HS baseball players. ~5% play in college. **100% of the parents wish they could.** That's the demand-side flywheel — if Rostr surfaces an athlete to a real college coach, the parent will pay anything. The coach didn't pay; the program didn't pay; the parent did.

The strategic insight: **build for the coach, charge the parent, monetize the recruiter.** Three customers, one product, layered pricing.

### 3c. Tech foundations (the boring stuff that matters)

- **Stack:** Next.js 14 App Router + Supabase (Postgres + Auth + RLS) + Anthropic Claude Haiku 4.5 + Vercel
- **38 migrations applied** to the linked remote DB. Idempotent patterns, defense-in-depth (RLS + app-layer + DB triggers for verified data).
- **Service-layer pattern** with Zod validation on all mutations.
- **Rate-limited AI calls** (5/min, 20/hour per coach) — Anthropic spend ceiling.
- **Demo guard** short-circuits every mutation in `/demo/*` so prospects can't break anything.
- **Public surfaces hardened:** `/p/[handle]`, `/g/[id]`, `/api/ical/[programId]` — all enforce opt-in privacy at the SQL view layer.
- **PWA + mobile polish.** App actually feels native on iPhone Safari. The dugout matters.

The technical floor is in. The business floor is not.

## 4. The honest gap analysis

Where Rostr is *not* yet:

### 4a. Distribution gaps (the biggest)

- **Zero installed base.** A pilot of one means a sample of one. Nothing is truly stress-tested until 10+ programs are running real seasons on it.
- **No paid customer.** Monetization machinery (Stripe, billing, plans, trial → paid conversion) is not built. Per CLAUDE.md scope rules, was deliberately out of pilot scope.
- **No outbound motion.** No coach-acquisition channel exists today. No SDR, no demo-request flow, no pricing page, no testimonial wall.
- **No inbound SEO.** Marketing site is one page. No blog, no comparison content vs. GameChanger, no "best practice planner for HS baseball" content.

### 4b. Product gaps (real but smaller)

- **Mobile field-test on a real iPhone in real conditions.** App is mobile-first by spec but hasn't been stress-tested in the dugout in low light with cold hands.
- **Bulk message / email blast.** Inbox UI exists; no SendGrid/Twilio integration to actually send to 30 parents at once. Coaches will ask for this in week 2.
- **GameChanger / MaxPreps import.** A coach with 5 years of GC data won't switch unless their history comes with them. No import path today.
- **Real-time multi-coach scoring.** Current live scoring is single-user. Two assistant coaches scoring the same game from different phones is not yet supported (Supabase realtime would handle it).
- **Video upload + native highlight reels.** Player profile accepts URLs (YouTube/Hudl/IG) but doesn't host video. The next obvious step (S3/Cloudflare Stream) is unbuilt.
- **Concurrent-edit handling on lineup builder.** Last-write-wins. Two coaches editing the same lineup simultaneously will lose work.
- **Roster CSV / PDF export** for season recaps and parent communications. Trivial to build, not built.
- **Web Push notifications.** PWA is installed; no push notifications wired. Game-day reminders to parents would drive engagement.
- **AI Assistant for athletes.** Built behind a flag; off until privacy review.
- **Training programs feature surface.** Flag exists; no implementation.

### 4c. Verification + recruiting gaps

- **Coach verification only covers highlights + prior-season stats.** Academics (GPA / SAT / ACT), contact info, and self-reported measurables are still pure player-reported with no per-field verify pattern.
- **No "Verified by Rostr" trust mark.** The verification chain is real but the brand around it is not. Recruiters won't pay for "coach said it's true" without a mark they can stand behind.
- **No recruiter outreach product.** The existing `/scout/*` system has lists + outreach but the new flag-gated `/scout/discover` only does search. Connecting "I found Alex" → "I want to send Alex a question" requires the messaging gate that's deliberately deferred.
- **No college coach onboarding.** Recruiter accounts are theoretical until an actual college coach signs up. We don't know if the search shape is right.

### 4d. Business / legal gaps

- **No payment infrastructure.** Stripe not integrated.
- **No multi-organization model.** A school district with 12 programs (V/JV/F across baseball + softball + soccer + …) can't be billed as one entity.
- **No FERPA / COPPA legal review.** Privacy gates are in code; the legal posture isn't formalized. Public schools will ask. The privacy/terms pages are placeholder substance.
- **No DPA template** for school districts.
- **No SOC2.** Doesn't matter for HS pilots; matters when colleges or districts buy.

### 4e. The single biggest gap

**Nobody knows Rostr exists.** Every other gap is solvable with engineering. This one is solved by walking into a coach's office and not leaving until the demo is over.

## 5. The market (sized honestly)

### Baseball

- **~16,000 US high schools** field a baseball program. Source: NFHS participation surveys.
- **~488,000 HS baseball players** (2023-24 NFHS).
- Average program runs ~30 players × 2-3 levels.
- **College recruiting funnel:** ~7% of HS baseball players play in some form of college baseball; ~2% at NCAA D1.
- **Parent willingness-to-pay:** middle-class baseball parent already spends $3-12k/year on travel ball, equipment, lessons. Spending $10/mo for the platform that surfaces their kid to colleges is a rounding error.

### Adjacent sports (sport-agnostic data layer means cheap expansion)

| Sport | HS programs | HS players | Expansion priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Football | ~14,000 | ~1.0M | High (huge market, but Hudl is entrenched) |
| Basketball (M+F) | ~18,000 each | ~540k + ~400k | High (big audience, fragmented tools) |
| Soccer (M+F) | ~12,000 each | ~460k + ~390k | Medium (expanding) |
| Volleyball (F) | ~16,000 | ~470k | High (underserved by tools, parent-heavy) |
| Lacrosse | ~3,500 | ~110k | High (affluent parents, recruiting-heavy) |
| Softball | ~16,000 | ~360k | Easy (basically same product as baseball) |

**Total addressable HS coaches in the US: ~250k+.** Median coach manages ~50 players. That's ~12-15 million HS athletes whose data could flow through one platform.

### Comparable companies / valuations (for context)

- **Hudl** — $400M revenue, 200k+ teams, $1B+ valuation. Started in football coaching video. Took ~15 years.
- **GameChanger** — owned by DICK'S Sporting Goods. Dominant in HS baseball/softball scorekeeping. Acquired for ~$22M in 2016. Now arguably worth $500M+ as an embedded feature of DICK'S retail funnel.
- **TeamSnap** — $50M+ revenue, 20M+ users. Team management for youth/rec sports. Different segment but same parent-engagement playbook.
- **Sportlyzer / SportsEngine** (NBC Sports owned) — multi-sport ops, valued in hundreds of millions.

The category supports $100M+ outcomes. The question is which slice you take.

## 6. Where the money is — a layered monetization plan

The business is three layers stacked on the same product. Each layer becomes possible only after the one below it works.

### Layer 1 — The Program Plan (coach pays)

**Who pays:** Head coach or athletic director, billed to the program / school.
**Price:** $49–149/month per program, or $499–999/year.
**What they get:** Everything in the live pilot product today. Roster, practice planner, tryouts, live scoring, schedule, AI helper, parent comms.
**Conversion path:** 30-day free trial → pilot the next tryout → annual contract before the season.

**Math at scale:**
- 1,000 programs × $99/mo = **$1.2M ARR**
- 5,000 programs × $99/mo = **$5.9M ARR**
- 16,000 programs × $99/mo (baseball saturation) = **$19M ARR**
- Add softball + football + basketball + soccer + volleyball + lacrosse: ~5x baseball TAM = **$95M ARR ceiling on Layer 1 alone.**

This layer alone is a real business. It's the floor.

### Layer 2 — The Family Plan (parent pays)

**Who pays:** Parent of an athlete on a Rostr-enrolled program.
**Price:** $7–15/month per athlete, billed to the parent. (Or $59/year.)
**What they get:**
- Full access to their athlete's verified profile editor (highlights, prior stats, contact, socials)
- The right to opt the profile public to recruiters
- Early-access to new player-side AI features (training plan generation, college matching suggestions)
- Premium analytics on their athlete's development trends
- Direct messaging to recruiters who view the profile
- Showcase event integration ("Find showcases that fit Alex's profile")

**Why it works:** The parent is the buyer with the strongest emotion in the funnel. Their kid's college future is in the balance. $10/mo is less than one batting lesson.

**Math at scale:**
- 30% parent attach rate × 1,000 programs × 50 players × $10/mo = **$1.8M ARR**
- 30% × 5,000 × 50 × $10 = **$9M ARR**
- 30% × 16,000 × 50 × $10 = **$28.8M ARR**

This layer ALONE could be larger than Layer 1 if attach rate hits. And it's all incremental on the same coach base.

### Layer 3 — The Scout / Recruiter Plan (the big margin)

**Who pays:** College baseball programs, independent recruiting services, agencies, scouting orgs, eventually MLB clubs (for amateur scouting) and NIL collectives.
**Price tiers:**
- **Individual recruiter:** $99/mo for search + watchlists
- **College program:** $499–1,499/mo for unlimited search, multi-recruiter seats, saved searches with new-match alerts
- **Recruiting service / agency:** $2,000–10,000/mo for white-label dashboards, bulk export, API access
- **Data licensing tier:** custom pricing for analytics firms / MLB clubs that want bulk verified-data feeds

**What they get:**
- Search the verified-data layer (Scout Discovery v1 / Signal Score)
- Saved searches with alerts when new athletes match
- Outreach tools (already built) — DM the athlete + parent
- Per-athlete deep dive: full verified history, stats, video, measurables
- Analytics: conversion rates, response rates, list curation
- Integration into existing recruiting CRMs (TeamSnap Recruit, FieldLevel, etc.)

**Why it works:** Recruiters today fly to showcases at $5k a pop to evaluate 200 kids in a weekend. If Rostr can hand them a sortable, filterable, verified, ranked list of every D1-prospective junior in their region, they'll pay.

**Math at scale:**
- 350 NCAA D1 baseball programs × $999/mo = **$4.2M ARR** from D1 alone
- Add ~280 D2 + 380 D3 + ~250 NAIA + ~430 JUCO programs at $499/mo = **+$8.0M ARR**
- Add ~200 independent recruiting services × $2k/mo = **+$4.8M ARR**
- Add data licensing (3-5 analytics firms × $50k/year) = **+$200k–250k ARR**
- **Total Layer 3 ceiling for baseball alone: ~$17M ARR.**

Multiply by sport. Football college recruiting alone is 5x bigger than baseball.

### Layer 4 — Adjacent monetization (after the flywheel spins)

- **Sponsorships from equipment brands.** Easton/Marucci/Wilson/Rawlings/Louisville want at-bat-context ad surfaces ("This BP rep brought to you by…"). $5-25k/program/year for premium brand placements. Risky to bring in too early; enormously lucrative once installed-base justifies it.
- **Showcase event partnerships.** Perfect Game, USABL, etc. pay for athlete data + booking flows. Per-event partnership fees + revenue share.
- **NIL / agent referrals.** When athletes commit, a small commission on NIL deals brokered through the platform. Don't build until you have the volume.
- **Insurance / camp / lessons marketplace.** Local hitting coach pays $50/lead to be in front of parents on the platform. Yelp model.
- **White-label for state athletic associations.** Texas UIL, California CIF, etc. License the platform to run state tryouts/playoffs/rankings. Six-figure annual contracts each.

**The realistic 5-year plan:** $20-40M ARR by year 5 across baseball + softball + 1-2 expansion sports, with the L3 recruiter tier as the high-margin differentiator. That's a $200-400M valuation business at SaaS multiples. Either acquire-target or independent.

## 7. Go-to-market: how you actually get to 1,000 paying programs

**The hardest question in the entire plan is this:** How does coach #11 hear about Rostr?

You don't beat GameChanger with features. You beat them by being the obvious choice for the next year's tryouts, when GameChanger doesn't even pretend to compete.

### Phase 1 — The 10-coach proof (next 6 months)

**Objective:** 10 paid annual contracts, hand-sold by the founder.

**Tactics:**

1. **Personal outreach to high-school baseball coaches in your geography.** Open a spreadsheet. Get every HS baseball coach within 100 miles. ~150 coaches. Email each one personally with a 60-second Loom demo. Aim for 30 demos, 10 closes.
2. **Sponsor a local coaches' clinic.** State HS baseball coaches' associations run annual conferences. $500-2k for a booth. A booth + a beer + a Loom = 50 coach conversations and 10 trial signups.
3. **Build the first case study from a public school nobody's heard of.** One trusted coach + one good season + one 90-second video = the social proof that unlocks the next 10.
4. **Don't pay for ads yet.** Cold ad spend before you have product-market fit is throwing money in a shredder.

**Success metric:** 10 paid programs by Aug 2026 (start of fall ball / pre-season tryouts in southern states).

### Phase 2 — The state-by-state run (next 12 months)

**Objective:** Dominate one state, then the next.

**Tactics:**

1. **Pick one state.** Probably the founder's home state. Optimize for density: every coach within a 200-mile radius is one phone call away.
2. **Hire one full-time field rep per state.** $60-80k base + commission. They drive to coaches' offices. They sit in the coach's office during tryouts. They become the coach's "tech guy". Founder-led sales doesn't scale; ex-coaches selling to coaches does.
3. **Build the State Leaderboard.** Public, free, data-flywheel feature: live state-wide rankings of verified athletes by class. Coaches LOVE seeing where their kid stacks up. Free to view, drives signups from non-Rostr programs who want their kids on the board.
4. **Recruit testimonial coaches.** Free or discounted lifetime access in exchange for case-study video, conference panels, name-on-the-website.
5. **Don't expand to other sports yet.** Depth in baseball before breadth across sports. Each sport has different politics; learn one before you take on three.

**Success metric:** 100 paid programs in one state by April 2027. State-saturation case study to use for the next state.

### Phase 3 — Multi-state expansion + L2 launch (months 18-30)

**Objective:** Repeatable state-by-state playbook + open the parent-pay tier.

**Tactics:**

1. **Productize the state-rep playbook.** Hiring template, comp plan, week-by-week activation cadence. Hire 5-8 reps in adjacent states.
2. **Launch the Family Plan.** Parents who want their kid's profile public have to opt in AND pay $7-10/mo. The opt-in is the moment of intent. Bundle a free trial with the program plan so coaches drive parent signups.
3. **Ship the verification trust mark.** "Rostr Verified" badge on public profiles. Coach has to verify. Marketing pages prominently feature it. Recruiters start to recognize it.
4. **Start collecting recruiter waitlist.** Every college coach who emails about an athlete profile gets put on a waitlist for the Scout tier.
5. **Add softball.** Same product, ~zero engineering cost (sport-agnostic data layer is real). Doubles the market overnight.

**Success metric:** 500 paid programs across 3-5 states + 5,000 paying parent accounts + 200 college coaches on the recruiter waitlist by Oct 2027.

### Phase 4 — Recruiter tier launch (months 30-42)

**Objective:** Open Layer 3 and prove it makes more revenue than Layers 1+2 combined.

**Tactics:**

1. **Cold-email every NCAA D1, D2, D3 baseball coach** in the country. (~1,400 contacts publicly listed.) "We have verified data on 50,000 HS players. Here's a 5-min demo."
2. **Co-launch with one or two well-known college programs.** Their endorsement is worth more than 100 cold emails.
3. **Partner with Perfect Game, Prep Baseball Report.** Don't fight them; be the connective tissue between their event data and HS coach data.
4. **Build the New Match alert system.** A college coach saves a search ("LHP, '27 grad, 80+ velo, 4.0 GPA, midwest"). Every new match → push notification. This becomes the daily-active recruiter habit.
5. **Charge.** Stop offering it free.

**Success metric:** $5M ARR from L3 by mid-year 3 (April 2028).

### Phase 5 — Adjacent sports + national saturation (months 42+)

**Objective:** Become the default HS sports OS.

**Tactics:**

1. **Add football.** The big prize. Hudl will fight; their tooling is dated. Coaches hate Hudl pricing.
2. **Add basketball.** Smaller per-program revenue but huge volume.
3. **Localize messaging by state.** Florida vs. California vs. Texas have different recruiting cultures. Marketing pages, coach networks, even product features should adapt.
4. **Target school districts and athletic departments.** 6-figure multi-program contracts.
5. **Open a college-coach hiring marketplace.** Once you have 100k athlete profiles, you can also be the place colleges find their next assistant coach. Adjacent monetization.

**Success metric:** $20M+ ARR by year 5. Default tool in 5,000 programs across 3 sports.

## 8. The competitive moat (why incumbents can't catch up)

| Threat | Why they don't win |
|---|---|
| **GameChanger + DICK'S** | They sell scorebooks. They don't do tryouts, practice, comms, recruiting. Their UX is ~10 years old. They're locked into a parent-facing brand that doesn't extend. |
| **Hudl** | They sell video. They don't touch operations. They charge a fortune. They're football-first; baseball is an afterthought. |
| **TeamSnap** | They sell scheduling for rec/youth. They don't have the verified-data trust posture. They don't do recruiting. |
| **MaxPreps** | They aggregate published results. They don't generate verified data; they republish what coaches send. They're owned by CBS Sports — slow. |
| **A new VC-funded competitor** | The incumbent advantage in this category is the operational data flywheel. By the time a competitor builds a product as good as Rostr's day-of-tryouts UX, Rostr already has 5 years of verified data per athlete. That's not catchable. |

The moat is **time × verified data per athlete**. Every season Rostr runs in a program, that program's seniors graduate with a 4-year verified record. A competitor starting today has zero seniors with 4-year records for ~5 years.

The moat compounds. Start now.

## 10. The honest risks

- **You're a one-person team building against multi-billion-dollar incumbents.** If you don't get help (cofounder, advisor, capital, sales rep) within 12 months of pilot launch, the competitive window closes.
- **The recruiter monetization assumes coaches will keep using Rostr long enough to generate the verified data.** If churn is high in years 1-2, the L3 thesis collapses.
- **Privacy + youth sports is a regulatory minefield.** COPPA, FERPA, state-level student-data privacy laws. Don't ship recruiter outreach to under-13s without lawyers.
- **HS coaches are paid by the school, not by you. They have zero incentive to switch tools mid-season.** Sell into pre-season windows or you'll lose every deal.
- **AI feature creep.** Don't build the "AI Coach for Athletes" before the parent payment plan ships. Every AI feature is anthropic spend you can't recover unless someone is paying.
